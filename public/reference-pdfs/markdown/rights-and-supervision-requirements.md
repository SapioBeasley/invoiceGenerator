# rights and supervision requirements

## RIGHTS OF THE INTELLECTUALLY DISABLED

1. A right to day program and home services that help you learn new and familiar things and make your life interesting. You should be given services with the right amount of support to match your needs.

2. A right to being treated with respect, to have your needs kept private from the public and to not be treated roughly.

3. A right to participate in programs and services that are paid for by the state, no matter what your support needs are.

4. A right to prompt medical care and treatment.

5. A right to religious freedom and practice.

6. A right to be with others and to go into the community to do things that you need and would enjoy.

7. A right to physical exercise and recreational activities.

8. A right to be free from being hurt, held down, tied up, locked in a room, given too much medication, or left without someone to help you when you need it.

9. A right to be free from dangerous activities/treatment.

If you feel you are being denied any of the above rights, please tell someone you trust to help you. You may also call your Service Coordinator at ELARC at (626) 299-4700.

## REVIEW OF GENERAL SUPERVISION REQUIREMENTS

1. Job Coaches will provide supervision and support to their consumers during day program service hours. Supervision means that you can see them or quickly get to them to offer instruction support (assistance) and/or keep them safe.

2. Consumers may briefly leave the Job Coach’s visual line of sight, to use the restroom, explore a store aisle, work in a different area of a jobsite, or make a purchase on their own. Each Consumer’s Individual Training Plan offers guidelines on how much autonomy they can safely handle. Some capable individuals can handle doing many things unsupervised; others must be kept by your side as much as possible. Consumers will be instructed to discuss with their Job Coach where
they will be, how long they will be “out of sight”, and when and where they will rejoin the group. They will be thanked for informing the Job Coach and congratulated for following through on what they said they must do.

3. Job Coaches agree that they are “on duty” during lunch and two 10-minute rest periods, and are paid for that period of time. Job Coaches are encouraged to rest, eat and use the restroom whenever consumers do so. 

4. Consumers are not to be left in a parked car while the Job Coach enters a building. The exception may be at pick-ups/drop-offs, when a Consumer may be safer staying seated for several minutes while the Job Coach escorts other Consumers to/from the car. Lower several car windows during warm weather to ensure adequate ventilation is maintained in the cabin when the engine is off. <u>Never leave your car keys in the ignition. or leave the car without the parking brake on while a Consumer sits in your parked car.</u>

5. Job Coaches will be close by whenever a Consumer uses a public restroom. They can be either inside the restroom, or standing outside by the door. Job Coaches should make themselves aware of who else is inside the restroom. Whenever possible, encourage the Consumer to wait until community members or children have finished using the restroom before going in themselves. If the Coach must use the restroom while the Consumer’s wait, the Job Coach should ensure that they are seated comfortably nearby, with directions to remain seated and wait for the Job
Coach’s return. Individuals who are at high risk of wandering off, elopement or bothering others will be asked to wait inside the restroom with the Job Coach.

6. Job Coaches will discuss with the Program Administrator any consumers they have regarding supervision issues, including Consumer requests for more autonomy.
