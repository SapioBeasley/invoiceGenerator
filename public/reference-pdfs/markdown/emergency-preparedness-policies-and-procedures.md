# emergency preparedness policies and procedures

## Emergency Preparedness Policies and Procedures
It is the policy of the HDS program to maintain care and supervision of our consumers in the
event of natural disaster or other large emergency event, until they can safely be released to
responsible persons, such as family or caregivers, or first responders such as Fire, Police,
Hospital or evacuation shelter personnel when appropriate. To that end, an on-going effort is
made to prepare staff and consumers through routine emergency drills; periodic inventory and
replenishment of emergency supplies; and administrative review of compliance/areas in need
of improvement.

Emergency PreDaredness Drills. Emergency drills will be performed at minimum every six
months, with one drill specifically focused on responding to a major earthquake. Each staff
member will be given the “Emergency Drill Preparation” form to guide their group’s preparation
for a drill. The form contains an ability assessment to rate each consumer’s basic
understanding of how to respond to a particular emergency type, and their ability to
demonstrate how to get to a protected position. On the back of the form is “Responding to
Emergencies: Some Basic Rules”, that describes initial protective actions to take in the event
of fire, earthquake, car accident or breakdown (the “Show Me” section).
For example, when staff prepare to do an earthquake drill, they will discuss with their
consumers the “rules” for responding to an earthquake (e.g., DROP/COVER /HOLD ON). Staff
will demonstrate DROP/COVER/HOLD ON, and ask each consumer to also rehearse
DROP/COVER/HOLD ON. Research has shown that rehearsal of the skills needed to protect
oneself makes it more likely that you will use those skills in an actual emergency. Adaptations
for mobility-restricted individuals will be reviewed as needed.


On the “Emergency Drill Preparation” form, staff will rate each consumer’s ability to understand the “rules” discussed, as well as their ability to verbalize and/or demonstrate what protective skills are called for in the event of an earthquake. Consumers will also be rated on their ability to locate their personal “emergency pack” (see below, under Emergency Supplies) or l.Dicontact information placed in their wallet and hand it to someone who has asked for their emergency contacts. This information will be reviewed by HDS administrative staff to identify which consumers may require additional support or training to respond effectively to an emergency.
Staff will perform an emergency drill and complete an “Emergency Drill” form to document the results of the drill. The form also asks that they inventory the emergency supplies in their car and each consumers’ personal supplies, in order to identify items missing or in need of replacement. HDS administrative staff will review the drill report to identify which staff or consumers need supplies, and make arrangements to obtain those supplies as soon as possible.

Emergency Locations. Given that HDS services are community based, staff will use available
resources to seek shelter, supplies and support while they determine how best to safely
transport consumers to their care providers at home or (if unsafe to do so) to a local
evacuation center. Community centers, senior centers, city halls, libraries, Police and Fire
stations, churches and schools, shopping mall security stations, transit centers, etc., are
locations that are prevalent, familiar to staff and consumers, and are likely to contain trained
personnel (often with radio contact to first responders) that can share information and
resources.

Consumer dayack/tote bag. HDS routinely recommends that consumers and their care
providers prepare a personal daypack, tote bag or large purse that they bring to a program that
contains a refillable water bottle, their lunch and/or snacks, and personal entertain-ment items
(magazines, radio, cell phone, etc.). Hygiene items (diapers, wipes, sanitary napkins) and
additional clothing items are included as appropriate to consumer needs.
HDS staff will place a folded copy of the consumer’s face sheet/consent for medical treatment
inside of a zip-lock plastic bag (“emergency pack”). Care providers will be asked to provide
one day’s worth of medications preferably in pharmacy labeled childproof containers for
program use in case of emergency. Such medications would ideally fit into the “emergency
pack” along with the face sheet/consent for medical treatment, and be secured inside of a
daypack/tote bag zippered pocket.
Consumers are encouraged to carry their own daypack/tote bag with them as much as
possible, especially when using public transportation. If a consumer repeatedly refuses to use
a daypack/tote bag, or there are good reasons not to do so (for example, a tendency to lose
things) the “emergency pack” may be carried by the Job Coach in their daypack.
Capable consumers may opt to carry l.D., medical information and emergency contact
numbers on a card in their wallet, rather than carry a face sheet. Those who carry a personal
cell phone will be encouraged to enter their own “ICE” (In Case of Emergency) contact phone
number, as well as phone numbers for the HDS office, their Job Coach’s work cell number, and
other emergency contacts.


If the request for one day’s worth of prescribed medications is repeatedly ignored or formally refused by the consumer’s care provider, this will be documented in a letter and sent to the service coordinator within 60 days of the initial request by the program.
Staff daypack/tote bag. Staff are required to carry a fully-charged program issued cell phone, HDS office business cards and a duplicate “emergency pack” containing face sheets for assigned and substitute consumers, as well as consumer medications (when necessary) in their personal daypack, tote bag or purse while they are involved in program activities in the community.
Emergency Supplies/Equipment. Each HDS staff assigned to provide consumer care is required to maintain basic emergency supplies that are kept in the staff vehicle, as well as carried on the person of each member of a program group. These supplies are intended to meet the immediate needs of consumers and staff during the first 24 hours after a major disaster while an effort is made to transport consumers home or (if determined unsafe to do so) to a local evacuation center, where additional supplies and support can be accessed.
Other supplies. Staff are required to keep in their passenger car trunk the following supplies: a first aid kit, a minimum of one gallon of drinking water, emergency snack foods (energy/granola bars), a battery powered flashlight, auto fire extinguisher, and a local map. These supplies are purchased by the program, and will be inventoried by administrative staff during vehicle checks
twice a year and by direct care staff in preparation for an emergency drill twice a year.
Communication with Care Providers. HDS staff are responsible to contact their assigned consumers’ care providers or other designated emergency contacts directly by cell phone as soon as possible after a major emergency to coordinate how best to return the consumer to their care. For emergency purposes HDS staff may provide their work cell phone number to care providers, and program the work cell phone with care provider cell phone numbers for efficiency. (When phone service is overloaded or otherwise disrupted, text messaging sometimes can connect, which is the reason cell numbers are preferable in an emergency to landline phone numbers.) The “Disaster Response Plan” policy will be given to care providers immediately after HDS conducts its annual in-service training on the subject.
